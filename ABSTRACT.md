Character Design Document

Bob — The Little Knight

Role



Bob is the game's protagonist.



He is a tiny knight whose courage is infinitely greater than his size.



Throughout the adventure he never becomes physically stronger than his enemies. Instead, he overcomes impossible challenges through determination, agility and perseverance.



The player should immediately perceive him as fragile, lovable and underestimated.



His silhouette, animations and expressions should constantly reinforce that he is the smallest character in the world while simultaneously being its greatest hero.



Art Style



Create Bob using a cute stylized 2D art style.



Use only flat colors with flat cel-shaded shadows.



No gradients.



No realistic textures.



Simple geometric shapes with clean silhouettes.



Rounded proportions.



Every animation should emphasize personality rather than realism.



The overall style should resemble a high-quality indie action game with charming visuals.



Silhouette



Bob is extremely small.



Almost every boss should appear between three and five times larger than him.



His silhouette must be recognizable even in complete darkness.



The most recognizable elements are:



oversized medieval helmet

tiny body

short cape

small stick sword with a single leaf

oversized gloves

oversized boots



His proportions should make him feel slightly toy-like without becoming cartoonish.



Body Proportions



Height:

Very short.



Head:

Large compared to his body.



Torso:

Small.



Arms:

Short but expressive.



Legs:

Short and agile.



Hands:

Slightly oversized to improve animation readability.



Feet:

Large armored boots that make his stance feel stable.



Overall proportions should maximize readability during fast combat.



Helmet



Bob never removes his helmet.



It completely defines his identity.



The helmet is inspired by medieval great helms but simplified into smooth, rounded shapes.



No decorative horns.



No wings.



No spikes.



The visor has no visible eye holes.



Instead, the opening is filled with complete darkness.



The player never sees his face.



The darkness inside the helmet should feel mysterious rather than frightening.



No glowing eyes.



No visible pupils.



Only an endless shadow.



This mystery should remain unresolved throughout the game.



Armor



Bob wears a classic medieval knight armor inspired by traditional storybook knights.



The armor is composed of smooth, rounded steel plates with clean and simple shapes. Every piece should look polished, sturdy and well maintained without appearing luxurious or overly detailed.



His helmet is the most recognizable part of his design. It resembles a simplified medieval great helm with a rounded dome, a protective faceplate and three narrow vertical visor slits. However, no eyes are ever visible. Behind the visor there is only an endless, deep shadow, giving the impression that no face exists beneath the helmet.



The pauldrons are large, rounded and smooth, making his silhouette immediately recognizable while preserving the cute proportions.



His torso is protected by a polished silver chestplate worn over a simple red knight's tunic that extends slightly below the armor, adding warmth and personality to his appearance.



He wears oversized rounded gauntlets and sturdy armored boots with exaggerated proportions to enhance readability during gameplay.



A wide black leather belt with a large square golden buckle wraps around his waist, helping visually separate the upper and lower body.



Small brown leather straps and simple metal rivets hold the armor together, reinforcing that everything is functional rather than decorative.



The overall armor should feel like that of a humble royal knight rather than a legendary champion.



No spikes.



No horns.



No glowing runes.



No magical ornaments.



The design should communicate innocence, determination and simplicity.

Colors



Primary Colors



Bright polished silver armor.

Soft crimson-red tunic beneath the armor.

Matte dark gray visor interior filled with complete darkness.

Black leather belt.



Secondary Colors



Warm golden belt buckle.

Warm brown leather straps.

Dark brown tree branch.

Fresh vibrant green leaf growing from the branch.



Accent Colors



Very small gold accents on the sword belt and armor joints to subtly reinforce his connection to the kingdom.



Avoid highly saturated colors.



Keep the palette soft, warm and visually charming.



The silver should appear bright and clean, contrasting beautifully against the red tunic and the tiny green leaf on his humble weapon.

Personality Through Animation



Bob almost never appears afraid.



Instead, he constantly projects determination.



Idle animation:



Small breathing motion.



Occasionally adjusts his grip on the branch.



Looks toward nearby enemies.



Run:



Fast.



Energetic.



Small but confident strides.



Attack:



Quick.



Precise.



No unnecessary spinning.



Every movement is economical.



Victory:



Raises his branch.



Looks toward the sky.



Small confident nod.



Never celebrates excessively.



Death:



Falls slowly.



The branch slips from his hand.



His helmet remains facing forward.



Emotional Details



Bob carries a small leather pouch attached to his belt.



Inside are his greatest treasures:



A worn photograph of Almohadita.



A small toy once used by the cat.



Several keepsakes collected throughout their life together.



The player rarely sees these objects directly, but they appear during important story moments.



These memories are what give Bob the strength to continue.



Character Expression



Because his face is never visible, all emotion must be communicated through:



Body language



Helmet angle



Movement



Timing



Pose



Camera framing



Players should still understand exactly what Bob feels despite never seeing his face.



Gameplay Personality



Bob is fast.



Extremely agile.



Low health.



High mobility.



Precise attacks.



Very short attack range.



Success comes from patience and mastering enemy patterns rather than dealing massive damage.



Every victory should feel earned.



Symbolism



Bob represents perseverance.



His tiny size symbolizes how insignificant one individual may appear.



His branch symbolizes that true strength does not come from weapons.



His hidden face allows every player to imagine themselves beneath the helmet.



His unwavering determination is what ultimately makes him the greatest knight in the kingdom.











**THE CAT KEEPER**

The Cat Keeper (Concept Art)



Style



Cute 2D fantasy game character with clean, simple shapes and strong silhouettes. Flat colors only, with flat cel-shaded shadows. No gradients, no textures, no outlines that are too thick. Friendly, charming art style similar to a modern indie action game. Armor is stylized rather than realistic, using oversized shapes and rounded proportions. The design should feel heroic and elegant instead of terrifying.



Overall silhouette



A massive human knight standing nearly three times taller than the protagonist.



He has extremely broad shoulders, a large chest, thick armored legs and an imposing but noble posture. His oversized greatsword is almost as tall as he is, immediately communicating overwhelming strength.



His long black cape creates a large triangular silhouette behind him, making him instantly recognizable.



Despite his size, his proportions remain clean and readable.



Face



A middle-aged Caucasian man with short black hair and sharp black eyes.



His expression is calm, serious and confident rather than angry.



His face shows the scars of countless battles:



slightly crooked nose

one long scar crossing his cheek

subtle deformation around one eyebrow

small facial scars



He still looks handsome and honorable despite the injuries.



No beard.



Strong jaw.



Determined gaze.



Armor



Bright polished silver armor inspired by medieval fantasy knights.



The armor should feel prestigious instead of bulky.



Large rounded pauldrons.



Heavy chestplate with elegant engraved details.



Massive armored gauntlets.



Wide armored boots.



Everything should have smooth, readable shapes without excessive ornamentation.



The armor reflects light using only flat highlights.



Cape



A long heavy black cape attached beneath the pauldrons.



The cape hangs almost to the ground.



It moves slowly, emphasizing the character's disciplined nature.



Weapon



A gigantic two-handed greatsword.



Very thick blade.



Simple crossguard.



Long grip.



The weapon should look incredibly heavy.



No magical glow.



No exaggerated fantasy spikes.



It should resemble the sword of the kingdom's greatest champion.



Colors



Primary:



Bright silver armor

Matte black cape



Secondary:



Dark leather straps

Brown grip



Skin:



Fair skin



Hair:



Black



Eyes:



Black



Accent color:



Very subtle royal blue details engraved into the armor to represent loyalty to the kingdom.



Personality through design



He should immediately communicate:



unwavering loyalty

discipline

dignity

confidence

honor



He does not look evil.



He should look like someone protecting what he truly believes is the world's last hope.



The player should feel respect before fear.



Pose



Standing perfectly upright.



Greatsword resting on the ground.



One hand holding the hilt.



Shoulders squared.



Looking directly toward the player.



Calm.



Confident.



Prepared.



Almost welcoming the duel.



Phase 2 Design



At 40% health, the knight violently tears off his armor.



The polished champion disappears.



Underneath is an enormous muscular warrior covered with scars.



His black cape is ripped.



His hair becomes messy.



His posture changes from disciplined to animalistic. 



His facial expression becomes one of unbearable agony.



His mouth is open in a silent scream.



His eyes are bloodshot and unfocused, as if witnessing multiple realities at once.



The fractures in time are affecting his mind.



His sword stance becomes wild and unpredictable.



He still wields the same colossal blade, but no longer with elegant technique.



Instead, every swing feels desperate, brutal and uncontrolled.



The contrast between the composed first phase and the savage second phase should immediately tell the player that his mind has completely collapsed.

Combat Style



The Cat Keeper is the ultimate test of everything the player has learned throughout the game.



He is a master swordsman whose combat style revolves around discipline, precision and absolute control.



Unlike previous bosses, he has almost no wasted movement. Every attack is intentional, every step controls space and every decision is calculated.



He is not merely powerful—he is technically superior.



The fight should demand complete mastery of the combat system, rewarding patience, spacing, timing and pattern recognition.



Players who rely on luck, panic dodging or button mashing should be defeated almost immediately.



Despite his overwhelming difficulty, every attack remains fair, readable and avoidable through skill.



The player should feel that every death teaches something new.



Victory should feel earned rather than granted.



General Behavior



The Cat Keeper constantly pressures the player without becoming reckless.



He walks with slow confidence, forcing the player to make the first mistake.



He prefers medium range but quickly closes any excessive distance.



He actively adapts to the player's positioning instead of blindly following predetermined patterns.



He rarely repeats the same combo twice in succession.



He intentionally delays some attacks to punish players who dodge purely from memory.



He occasionally interrupts his own offense to reposition, forcing the player to constantly adapt.



He recognizes opportunities created by the player's mistakes and immediately capitalizes on them.



He never feels random, but he is highly unpredictable.



The player should never feel completely comfortable.



Basic Combat



The Cat Keeper's swordsmanship is nearly flawless.



Every attack flows naturally into the next.



Every combo can transition into multiple endings.



Recovery windows exist, but only if the player correctly identifies the complete sequence.



Premature aggression is heavily punished.



Basic Combo



A disciplined sequence of three devastating sword strikes.



Each attack has slightly different timing, preventing rhythmic dodging.



The third strike may naturally transition into one of several follow-up options.



Players who immediately counterattack without confirming the combo has truly ended are punished.



Advanced Combo



A sequence of five heavy attacks demonstrating the full extent of the knight's mastery.



Attack timing constantly alternates between slow and surprisingly fast swings.



Several attacks slightly adjust their direction to catch careless movement.



The final attack leaves a noticeable opening—but only after the entire sequence has completely finished.



Greedy players attempting to interrupt the combo are punished.



Special Attacks



Special attacks are fully integrated into his swordsmanship rather than existing as isolated abilities.



Every special attack serves to deny a defensive habit the player has likely developed throughout the game.



No attack exists purely for spectacle.



Swift Slash



The Cat Keeper launches himself upward with a powerful rising slash before crashing back down.



The landing timing varies slightly each time, forcing players to react instead of memorizing.



The impact creates a powerful shockwave around the landing point.



After landing, he recovers only briefly before reassessing the player's position.



Assault



The knight lowers his stance before charging forward with explosive speed.



Rather than always traveling the same distance, he adjusts the charge depending on the player's location.



If the attack misses, recovery is short enough that careless punish attempts are dangerous.



Players must commit only when certain the attack has fully ended.



Seismic Strike



The Cat Keeper drives his sword into the ground, sending a massive shockwave across the battlefield.



The attack controls space rather than directly targeting the player.



The shockwave forces repositioning while limiting safe areas of the arena.



He may immediately follow the shockwave with another attack if the player hesitates.



Difficulty Philosophy



The Cat Keeper is intentionally one of the hardest encounters in the game.



His challenge comes from mastery rather than unfairness.



He possesses:



Excellent spacing.

Exceptional attack variety.

Adaptive combo routing.

High damage.

Minimal recovery windows.

Constant pressure.

Extremely consistent punish game.



However, he never breaks the rules established by the combat system.



Every attack has:



a visible startup,

consistent hitboxes,

reliable dodge timing,

and logical recovery.



The player should always understand why they failed.



Every defeat should improve the player's understanding of the fight.



The encounter should feel like fighting the kingdom's greatest warrior—not an impossible boss.



Phase Two



At 40% health, discipline gives way to madness.



The temporal fractures shatter the knight's mind.



His refined technique collapses into violent instinct.



His attack speed increases dramatically.



Recovery windows become significantly shorter.



He begins chaining attacks that were previously impossible.



Several special attacks can now be linked together.



Swift Slash no longer exhausts him.



Assault is performed twice in rapid succession.



Seismic Strike produces larger shockwaves that occupy more of the arena.



His movement becomes faster and far less predictable.



He relentlessly pressures the player, leaving almost no time to recover.



Despite this aggression, every attack remains visually readable.



The difficulty comes from surviving the relentless pace while maintaining mechanical precision.



The player should feel as though they are no longer dueling a disciplined champion, but witnessing the tragic collapse of the greatest swordsman the kingdom has ever known.



Final Combat Experience



The Cat Keeper should feel like the game's true mechanical exam.



Every mechanic introduced throughout the adventure is tested simultaneously:



Positioning.

Dodge timing.

Stamina management.

Patience.

Pattern recognition.

Adaptability.

Risk assessment.

Precision under pressure.



The player should finish this battle believing they have overcome the greatest warrior in the kingdom.











Character Design Document

Almohadita

Role



Almohadita is the emotional heart of the entire game.



He is not a companion, a playable character or an ally during combat.



He never attacks.



He never fights.



He never intentionally uses his powers as weapons.



Instead, he exists as the objective that motivates every action taken by the player.



Everything the protagonist does is driven by the desire to bring him home.



The player should feel that Almohadita is worth saving long before understanding his true importance.



Art Style



Create Almohadita using the same stylized 2D aesthetic as the rest of the game.



Flat colors only.



Flat cel-shaded shadows.



Simple geometric shapes.



Rounded proportions.



Cute and expressive without becoming overly cartoonish.



His design should immediately communicate warmth, innocence and comfort.



Overall Silhouette



Almohadita is a small domestic tuxedo cat.



His silhouette should be instantly recognizable.



Round head.



Small triangular ears.



Compact body.



Short legs.



Large fluffy tail.



His proportions should emphasize softness rather than realism.



Even sitting still he should look peaceful.



Fur Pattern



His fur is predominantly white.



Soft gray patches cover:



The top of his head.



His back.



The upper half of his tail.



Part of one ear.



The gray pattern should feel natural and asymmetrical.



His chest, belly, muzzle and paws remain white.



Face



Large warm orange eyes.



Small pink nose.



Tiny whiskers.



Soft rounded cheeks.



His expressions are subtle rather than exaggerated.



Most of the time he simply observes the world with curiosity.



His gaze should always feel gentle.



Colors



Primary Colors



Warm white fur.



Soft neutral gray markings.



Bright amber-orange eyes.



Light pink nose.



Secondary Colors



Soft pink paw pads.



Very subtle warm shadows.



Avoid saturated colors.



Keep the palette warm, soft and comforting.



Personality Through Animation



Almohadita behaves exactly like a normal cat.



He stretches after sleeping.



Slowly blinks.



Tilts his head when confused.



Looks at insects.



Yawns.



Rolls onto his back.



Flicks his ears toward sounds.



Chases floating leaves.



Sleeps in sunbeams.



Curiously sniffs unfamiliar objects.



None of these behaviors should appear forced.



He should feel alive.



Presence Throughout the Game



Although physically absent for most of the adventure, Almohadita should constantly remind the player why the journey exists.



His presence appears through:



Old photographs.



Abandoned toys.



Sleeping places.



Scratch marks.



Loose white and gray fur.



Memories.



Dreams.



Objects Bob still carries.



The world itself should quietly remind the player that someone precious is missing.



True Nature



Unknown to everyone—including Almohadita himself—he is not an ordinary cat.



Long before the kingdom existed, an ancient deity sealed an immeasurable source of primordial power inside the purest vessel it could create.



Rather than choosing a weapon, a king or a divine relic, the deity chose something the world would never fear:



A small, innocent cat.



Generations passed.



Civilizations rose and fell.



The vessel continued living ordinary lives.



Eventually that vessel became Almohadita.



He has lived his entire life believing himself to be nothing more than a normal house cat.



He has no knowledge of the power sleeping within him.



His innocence is what keeps the seal stable.



Cosmic Power



Hidden inside Almohadita exists a force capable of manipulating:



Time.



Space.



Dimensions.



Parallel realities.



Possible futures.



Forgotten timelines.



However, Almohadita cannot consciously control these abilities.



They manifest instinctively whenever the seal surrounding the power becomes unstable.



The distortions affecting the kingdom are not caused by Almohadita himself.



They are the consequence of others attempting to forcibly extract the power contained within him.



Every failed attempt weakens the ancient seal.



As reality fractures, different timelines begin colliding into one another.



Entire places briefly overlap.



Multiple versions of history begin existing simultaneously.



This growing instability eventually reaches the minds of those closest to the source, explaining why several late-game bosses slowly lose their sanity.



Connection With Bob



Although separated by great distances, Almohadita unconsciously senses Bob's determination.



Without understanding how, fragments of his hidden power begin searching through infinite timelines for ways to help his beloved friend.



Rather than fighting directly, he reaches across fractured realities and sends objects that once belonged to alternate versions of himself.



These become the magical amulets the player discovers throughout the adventure.



Each amulet carries a small fragment of another possible history.



Some belonged to worlds where Bob never left home.



Others come from timelines where the kingdom had already fallen.



Some originate from realities that no longer exist.



Every amulet is both a gameplay upgrade and a remnant of another universe.



Almohadita never realizes he is sending them.



He simply dreams of home.



Symbolism



Almohadita represents innocence.



Hope.



Home.



Memory.



Unconditional companionship.



The paradox of infinite power contained within absolute purity.



He is simultaneously the least dangerous being in existence and the single most important life in every reality.



His greatest strength is not the cosmic force sleeping inside him.



It is the bond he shares with Bob.



That bond is what allows hope to survive across countless collapsing timelines.





















Arena Design Document

General Philosophy



Every boss battle takes place inside a unique arena specifically designed around that encounter.



The arena is not merely a battlefield—it is a visual extension of the boss itself, telling part of the story before the fight even begins.



Each arena should immediately communicate the personality, history and emotional weight of its guardian.



Throughout the game, the arenas gradually reflect the slow collapse of the kingdom.



As the player approaches the source of the corruption, every location becomes increasingly damaged by the influence of The Unknown One.



The environments should visually tell the player:



"Reality itself is beginning to break."



Art Style



Create every arena using the same stylized 2D aesthetic as the characters.



Flat colors only.



Flat cel-shaded shadows.



Simple, readable geometry.



Large recognizable shapes.



No realistic textures.



No photorealistic lighting.



The environments should resemble illustrated storybook locations brought to life.



Despite the cute art style, the atmosphere should progressively become more melancholic and unsettling.



Gameplay Philosophy



Every arena is designed first for combat.



The battlefield must always prioritize gameplay readability.



The player should never lose visibility because of decorative elements.



The floor must clearly communicate safe and dangerous areas.



Background elements should never interfere with gameplay.



No visual clutter.



The player must always understand where they can move.



Progression Through the Game



The arenas gradually evolve alongside the story.



Rather than changing suddenly, each new arena shows another stage of the kingdom's decline.



The world slowly transitions through four visual states.



Early Game



The kingdom is still recognizable.



The environment feels peaceful.



Blue skies.



Healthy vegetation.



Warm sunlight.



Clean stone architecture.



Beautiful castles.



Colorful flowers.



Small rivers.



Birds.



Wind moving the grass.



The player feels they are exploring a kingdom that still remembers happier days.



Only subtle signs suggest that something is wrong.



Small black vines.



Tiny cracks.



Occasional strange shadows.



Mid Game



The corruption becomes visible.



Buildings begin collapsing.



Statues lose their faces.



Roads crack apart.



Vegetation begins dying.



The colors become slightly desaturated.



The sky grows cloudier.



Dark crystalline growths begin emerging from the ground.



Fragments of reality occasionally flicker.



The player begins to understand that the kingdom is slowly losing itself.



Late Game



Darkness has spread across nearly every location.



Entire buildings are broken.



Ancient bridges collapse.



Trees become twisted.



The sky remains permanently overcast.



Reality becomes unstable.



Small temporal fractures appear throughout the environment.



Background architecture occasionally shifts into different versions of itself.



Objects briefly disappear before returning.



Different timelines begin overlapping.



The player feels increasingly isolated.



Final Areas



Reality no longer behaves normally.



Entire sections of the environment belong to different eras simultaneously.



Ancient ruins merge with pristine castles.



Destroyed structures exist beside intact versions of themselves.



Floating debris hangs motionless in the air.



Gravity appears inconsistent.



The sky constantly changes.



Stars become visible during daylight.



The horizon bends unnaturally.



Pieces of architecture drift between dimensions.



The kingdom no longer resembles a physical place.



It has become a wound in reality itself.



Environmental Storytelling



Every arena should communicate its history without dialogue.



Examples include:



Broken weapons left behind.



Abandoned camps.



Damaged statues.



Old banners.



Collapsed bridges.



Ruined cat shrines.



Forgotten graves.



Children's toys.



Discarded armor.



Ancient murals.



Everything should quietly suggest what happened before the player arrived.



Environmental Animation



The world should never feel static.



Small environmental animations constantly reinforce atmosphere.



Grass moves with the wind.



Dust slowly drifts through sunlight.



Leaves fall naturally.



Rain occasionally forms puddles.



Broken banners wave gently.



Torches flicker.



Loose stones fall from damaged buildings.



Tiny particles float through corrupted areas.



Reality fractures pulse softly.



Floating debris slowly rotates.



These animations should remain subtle.



The environment should feel alive without distracting the player.



Arena Composition



Every arena follows the same visual structure.



Foreground



Only minimal decorative objects that never obstruct combat.



Main Platform



A large open fighting space designed for movement.



Midground



Architecture defining the location.



Background



Large environmental landmarks reinforcing the arena's identity.



Sky



Changes throughout the game to reflect the world's corruption.



Arena Size



Every arena should feel spacious enough for boss combat.



Players should always have room to:



Dodge.



Retreat.



Reposition.



Recover.



Bosses should never become trapped against walls.



Arena boundaries should feel natural.



Collapsed ruins.



Castle walls.



Ancient trees.



Cliffs.



Massive gates.



Never invisible barriers.



Visual Progression During Boss Battles



Some arenas evolve while the battle progresses.



Large impacts crack the floor.



Columns collapse.



Pieces of architecture fall away.



The sky darkens.



Reality fractures spread.



Dark energy slowly consumes the battlefield.



These changes should never alter gameplay unfairly.



Instead, they reinforce the emotional escalation of the encounter.



Color Palette



Early Game



Warm greens.



Soft blues.



Bright stone.



Golden sunlight.



Mid Game



Cool grays.



Dark greens.



Muted blues.



Late Game



Ash gray.



Deep black.



Dark violet.



Cold blue.



Final Areas



Black.



White.



Dark crimson.



Purple.



Fragments of impossible colors appearing inside reality fractures.



Relationship with The Unknown One



The corruption is not simply darkness.



It is reality being rewritten.



Wherever The Unknown One's influence spreads:



Time becomes unstable.



Space folds unnaturally.



Architectural styles merge.



Vegetation grows incorrectly.



Physics begins to fail.



The player should slowly realize that they are not merely watching the kingdom die.



They are watching existence itself become incompatible with reality.



Emotional Goal



Each new arena should leave the player with a different emotional impression.



Early arenas inspire curiosity.



Middle arenas inspire concern.



Late arenas inspire loneliness.



The final arenas inspire cosmic unease.



Despite the gradual destruction, every location should preserve traces of the kingdom's former beauty.



The player should constantly be reminded that this was once a peaceful land worth saving.

# Boss Design Document

## Overview

Bosses are the heart of **Ash & Purr**.

Every major encounter is designed to feel like a climactic duel against an overwhelming opponent rather than a simple obstacle.

Bosses are not damage sponges or scripted set pieces—they are fully realized characters with their own personalities, fighting styles, philosophies, arenas, and narrative significance.

Each boss represents a different aspect of the kingdom's downfall while simultaneously teaching or testing a specific combat skill.

By the end of the game, the player should remember every boss by name, personality, and fighting style.

---

# Design Philosophy

Every boss should answer one question:

> **"How can this encounter make the player feel incredibly small without ever feeling powerless?"**

Bosses should overwhelm the player through:

* Scale
* Presence
* Combat mastery
* Arena design
* Narrative importance

Never through unfair mechanics.

---

# Core Principles

Every boss must follow these principles.

## Fairness

Every attack must have:

* Clear anticipation.
* Readable animations.
* Accurate hitboxes.
* Consistent timing.
* Logical recovery windows.

The player should always understand why they were hit.

---

## Mechanical Identity

Every boss teaches something unique.

Examples include:

* Spacing
* Patience
* Precision dodging
* Delayed attacks
* Positioning
* Aggression control
* Risk management
* Pattern recognition

No two bosses should test the exact same skills.

---

## Narrative Identity

Bosses are not monsters placed at random.

Each one is:

* A guardian.
* A fallen hero.
* A corrupted champion.
* A tragic victim.
* A servant of The Unknown One.

Every encounter advances the story.

The player should learn about the world through combat rather than exposition.

---

## Visual Identity

Every boss should be instantly recognizable.

Each boss requires:

* Unique silhouette.
* Distinct color palette.
* Memorable introduction.
* Exclusive arena.
* Unique sound profile.
* Signature attacks.

Players should immediately know who they are fighting before a single attack begins.

---

# Boss Progression

Bosses should gradually increase in complexity rather than simply increasing health or damage.

Progression follows this structure:

### Early Bosses

Teach individual mechanics.

Examples:

* Basic dodge timing.
* Punishing greed.
* Safe punish windows.

---

### Mid Game Bosses

Require combining multiple skills.

Examples:

* Delayed attacks.
* Arena awareness.
* Combo recognition.

---

### Late Game Bosses

Demand mastery.

Players must:

* Read attacks instinctively.
* Adapt under pressure.
* Recognize subtle animation changes.
* Maintain composure.

---

### Final Bosses

Every mechanic learned throughout the game is tested simultaneously.

The challenge comes from execution rather than surprise.

---

# Boss Scale

Scale is one of the game's strongest visual storytelling tools.

General proportions:

* Early Bosses

  * Approximately 3× Bob's height.

* Mid Bosses

  * Approximately 4× Bob's height.

* Final Bosses

  * Approximately 5× Bob's height.

The enormous size difference reinforces Bob's vulnerability while making every victory feel extraordinary.

---

# Arena Philosophy

A boss arena is an extension of its guardian.

Nothing exists purely for decoration.

The arena should communicate:

* Personality.
* History.
* Emotional tone.
* Current state of the kingdom.

As the story progresses, arenas become increasingly unstable.

Architecture collapses.

Nature decays.

Reality fractures.

The environment should foreshadow the narrative long before it is explained.

---

# Boss Introductions

Boss introductions should create anticipation rather than spectacle.

General guidelines:

* Slow pacing.
* Minimal dialogue.
* Strong visual storytelling.
* Controlled camera movement.
* Memorable first impression.

The boss should appear calm.

Powerful opponents do not need to prove their strength.

Their presence alone should command respect.

---

# Combat Philosophy

Boss combat is rhythmic.

Players naturally enter the following loop:

Observe.

Dodge.

Analyze.

Punish.

Retreat.

Repeat.

Greed should always be punished.

Patience should always be rewarded.

Every boss should encourage deliberate play instead of constant aggression.

---

# Attack Design

Every attack must exist for a reason.

Attacks should challenge specific player habits.

Examples:

* Early dodge.
* Panic rolling.
* Overcommitting.
* Unsafe healing.
* Predictable positioning.

No attack exists only because it "looks cool."

Gameplay clarity always takes priority.

---

# Combo Design

Boss combos should feel intelligent.

Characteristics:

* Natural transitions.
* Variable timing.
* Multiple endings.
* Logical recovery.

Players should learn to identify when a combo has truly ended rather than attacking after every swing.

---

# Phase Design

Bosses may have multiple combat phases.

A phase transition should represent:

* Emotional change.
* Narrative progression.
* Mechanical evolution.

Not simply:

* More health.
* Faster attacks.
* Bigger numbers.

Each phase should tell part of the boss's story.

---

# Audio Design

Every boss has a unique sound identity.

Players should recognize attacks by sound alone.

Important attacks should begin with recognizable audio cues before becoming visually dangerous.

Silence is intentionally used before impactful attacks to increase tension.

Heavy bosses should sound heavy.

Elegant bosses should sound controlled.

Broken bosses should sound unstable.

---

# Difficulty Philosophy

Difficulty comes from:

* Pattern recognition.
* Timing.
* Positioning.
* Decision making.
* Precision.
* Consistency.

Never from:

* Random attacks.
* Hidden mechanics.
* Artificial damage.
* Unavoidable situations.

Every death should teach.

Every retry should improve the player's understanding.

# The Cat Keeper

## Role

The Cat Keeper is the penultimate boss and the greatest swordsman the kingdom has ever known.

He is the King's most loyal champion and the final guardian standing between Bob and Almohadita.

Believing he is protecting the kingdom from an ancient catastrophe, he fights Bob with absolute conviction, unaware that the true king has long since been replaced by The Unknown One.

Unlike many bosses, he is not evil.

He is a tragic hero whose loyalty has been manipulated.

---

## Narrative Purpose

The Cat Keeper represents:

* Loyalty.
* Honor.
* Duty.
* Sacrifice.

His battle is not a clash between good and evil.

It is a duel between two people trying to protect what they love.

His defeat marks the emotional climax of the story, revealing the tragic consequences of blind devotion.

---

## The Cat Keeper (Concept Art)

# Style

Cute 2D fantasy game character with clean, simple shapes and strong silhouettes. Flat colors only, with flat cel-shaded shadows. No gradients, no textures, and thin clean outlines. Friendly, charming art style similar to a modern indie action game. The character should look powerful and intimidating through scale rather than excessive detail. Armor is stylized with rounded proportions and oversized pieces while remaining elegant and highly readable.

# Overall silhouette

A gigantic armored paladin standing in a wide, grounded stance.

His body is extremely broad with massive shoulders, a thick torso and powerful armored legs, giving him the appearance of an immovable fortress.

He is completely covered by heavy armor, making him appear mysterious and anonymous.

His enormous black cape expands behind him, creating a large triangular silhouette that reinforces his commanding presence.

A colossal greatsword stands vertically in front of him, its tip planted firmly into the ground while both hands rest on the pommel.

His proportions are exaggerated but clean and easy to read, emphasizing strength over realism.

# Helmet

The helmet completely conceals his face.

Smooth medieval great helm with a narrow T-shaped visor.

No visible eyes.

No facial features.

No ornaments, horns or decorative crests.

The helmet is simple, symmetrical and imposing.

# Armor

Heavy full plate armor with a dark metallic finish.

The armor is almost black with subtle cool gray highlights.

Large rounded pauldrons.

Massive gauntlets.

Layered arm and leg plates.

Broad chestplate with simple geometric shapes.

Rounded knee guards.

Heavy armored boots.

The armor avoids excessive engravings or ornamentation, relying instead on strong shapes and proportions.

Every surface uses flat colors with simple cel-shaded shadows.

# Cape

A long flowing black cape attached beneath the shoulder armor.

The cape extends almost to the ground.

It naturally spreads behind the character, creating a dramatic silhouette.

Its movement is subtle, suggesting calm confidence rather than motion.

# Weapon

A gigantic two-handed greatsword.

Very thick rectangular blade.

Simple straight crossguard.

Long grip sized for both hands.

Dark metallic blade matching the armor.

The sword is planted into the ground.

Both hands rest on the pommel, emphasizing patience, discipline and overwhelming strength.

No magical effects.

No glowing runes.

No exaggerated fantasy decorations.

# Colors

### Primary:

Dark charcoal armor

Black cape

### Secondary:

Cool gray highlights

Dark steel sword

### Accent colors:

Very subtle silver reflections on the armor.

No bright colors.

The overall palette should feel restrained, elegant and intimidating.

# Personality through design

He should immediately communicate:

- immense strength
- discipline
- absolute confidence
- patience
- honor
- authority

He does not appear savage or monstrous.

He stands like an elite guardian or legendary champion waiting for a worthy opponent.

The player should feel respect before fear.

# Pose

Standing with legs apart in a stable combat stance.

The greatsword planted vertically into the ground.

Both hands resting calmly on the sword's pommel.

Shoulders squared.

Head facing forward.

Cape flowing gently behind him.

Completely still.

Silent.

Prepared.

As if patiently waiting for the duel to begin.

---

# Phase 2 Design

At 40% health, the knight violently tears off his armor.

The once composed paladin completely loses his composure.

His disciplined stance is replaced by violent, erratic movements.

His armor remains intact but becomes heavily fractured, with deep cracks spreading across every plate.

A crimson light erupts from the visor of his helmet.

Two concentrated beams of red energy burst from his eyes, suggesting an overwhelming force trapped within the armor.

His body constantly trembles as if struggling to contain the power consuming him.

He grips the same colossal greatsword, but no longer with elegance or discipline.

His attacks become reckless, brutal and driven purely by rage.

As he charges forward, the blade tears through the ground, throwing debris and dust into the air.

Small rocks are lifted by the intensity of his power.

The contrast with the first phase should immediately communicate that the noble champion has completely lost control while still remaining trapped inside his armor.

The contrast between the composed first phase and the savage second phase should immediately tell the player that his mind has completely collapsed.

---

# Combat Style

The Cat Keeper is the ultimate test of everything the player has learned throughout the game.

He is a master swordsman whose combat style revolves around discipline, precision and absolute control.

Unlike previous bosses, he has almost no wasted movement. Every attack is intentional, every step controls space and every decision is calculated.

He is not merely powerful—he is technically superior.

The fight should demand complete mastery of the combat system, rewarding patience, spacing, timing and pattern recognition.

Players who rely on luck, panic dodging or button mashing should be defeated almost immediately.

Despite his overwhelming difficulty, every attack remains fair, readable and avoidable through skill.

The player should feel that every death teaches something new.

Victory should feel earned rather than granted.

---

# General Behavior

The Cat Keeper constantly pressures the player without becoming reckless.

He walks with slow confidence, forcing the player to make the first mistake.

He prefers medium range but quickly closes any excessive distance.

He actively adapts to the player's positioning instead of blindly following predetermined patterns.

He rarely repeats the same combo twice in succession.

He intentionally delays some attacks to punish players who dodge purely from memory.

He occasionally interrupts his own offense to reposition, forcing the player to constantly adapt.

He recognizes opportunities created by the player's mistakes and immediately capitalizes on them.

He never feels random, but he is highly unpredictable.

The player should never feel completely comfortable.

---

# Basic Combat

The Cat Keeper's swordsmanship is nearly flawless.

Every attack flows naturally into the next.

Every combo can transition into multiple endings.

Recovery windows exist, but only if the player correctly identifies the complete sequence.

Premature aggression is heavily punished.

## Basic Combo

A disciplined sequence of three devastating sword strikes.

Each attack has slightly different timing, preventing rhythmic dodging.

The third strike may naturally transition into one of several follow-up options.

Players who immediately counterattack without confirming the combo has truly ended are punished.

## Advanced Combo

A sequence of five heavy attacks demonstrating the full extent of the knight's mastery.

Attack timing constantly alternates between slow and surprisingly fast swings.

Several attacks slightly adjust their direction to catch careless movement.

The final attack leaves a noticeable opening—but only after the entire sequence has completely finished.

Greedy players attempting to interrupt the combo are punished.

---

# Special Attacks

Special attacks are fully integrated into his swordsmanship rather than existing as isolated abilities.

Every special attack serves to deny a defensive habit the player has likely developed throughout the game.

No attack exists purely for spectacle.

## Swift Slash

The Cat Keeper launches himself upward with a powerful rising slash before crashing back down.

The landing timing varies slightly each time, forcing players to react instead of memorizing.

The impact creates a powerful shockwave around the landing point.

After landing, he recovers only briefly before reassessing the player's position.

## Assault

The knight lowers his stance before charging forward with explosive speed.

Rather than always traveling the same distance, he adjusts the charge depending on the player's location.

If the attack misses, recovery is short enough that careless punish attempts are dangerous.

Players must commit only when certain the attack has fully ended.

## Seismic Strike

The Cat Keeper drives his sword into the ground, sending a massive shockwave across the battlefield.

The attack controls space rather than directly targeting the player.

The shockwave forces repositioning while limiting safe areas of the arena.

He may immediately follow the shockwave with another attack if the player hesitates.

---

# Difficulty Philosophy

The Cat Keeper is intentionally one of the hardest encounters in the game.

His challenge comes from mastery rather than unfairness.

He possesses:

- Excellent spacing.
- Exceptional attack variety.
- Adaptive combo routing.
- High damage.
- Minimal recovery windows.
- Constant pressure.
- Extremely consistent punish game.

However, he never breaks the rules established by the combat system.

Every attack has:

- a visible startup,
- consistent hitboxes,
- reliable dodge timing,
- and logical recovery.

The player should always understand why they failed.

Every defeat should improve the player's understanding of the fight.

The encounter should feel like fighting the kingdom's greatest warrior—not an impossible boss.

---

# Phase Two

At 40% health, discipline gives way to madness.

The temporal fractures shatter the knight's mind.

His refined technique collapses into violent instinct.

His attack speed increases dramatically.

Recovery windows become significantly shorter.

He begins chaining attacks that were previously impossible.

Several special attacks can now be linked together.

Swift Slash no longer exhausts him.

Assault is performed twice in rapid succession.

Seismic Strike produces larger shockwaves that occupy more of the arena.

His movement becomes faster and far less predictable.

He relentlessly pressures the player, leaving almost no time to recover.

Despite this aggression, every attack remains visually readable.

The difficulty comes from surviving the relentless pace while maintaining mechanical precision.

The player should feel as though they are no longer dueling a disciplined champion, but witnessing the tragic collapse of the greatest swordsman the kingdom has ever known.

---

# Final Combat Experience

The Cat Keeper should feel like the game's true mechanical exam.

Every mechanic introduced throughout the adventure is tested simultaneously:

- Positioning.
- Dodge timing.
- Stamina management.
- Patience.
- Pattern recognition.
- Adaptability.
- Risk assessment.
- Precision under pressure.

The player should finish this battle believing they have overcome the greatest warrior in the kingdom.

## Emotional Goal

Players should finish the battle believing they have defeated the greatest warrior the kingdom has ever known.

Rather than feeling triumph over evil, they should feel admiration, sadness, and respect.

The Cat Keeper is not remembered because he was cruel.

He is remembered because he fought honorably until the very end.

---

# Final Boss Design Rule

Every future boss added to the game must satisfy four requirements:

1. Present a unique mechanical challenge.
2. Reinforce the narrative through combat.
3. Possess a memorable visual and audio identity.
4. Make the player feel like a tiny knight overcoming impossible odds through mastery rather than power.

If a boss cannot accomplish these four goals, it does not belong in **Ash & Purr**.

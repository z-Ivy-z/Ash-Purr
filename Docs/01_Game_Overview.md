# Game Overview

**Project:** Ash & Purr
**Genre:** 2D Arcade Action • Soulslike Combat • Boss Rush Adventure

---

# Vision Statement

**Ash & Purr** is a 2D arcade action game built around precise, skill-based combat inspired by the philosophy of Soulslike games. Rather than focusing on RPG progression or numerical power growth, the game challenges players to overcome seemingly impossible opponents through observation, timing, patience, and perseverance.

The player controls **Bob**, the smallest knight in the kingdom, armed with nothing more than a humble tree branch. Despite his lack of strength, armor, or legendary weapons, Bob embarks on a journey to rescue his beloved cat, **Almohadita**. What begins as a personal mission slowly reveals the collapse of an entire kingdom and the existence of an ancient force capable of reshaping reality.

The core experience is built around one idea:

> **A tiny knight can overcome impossible odds through determination, not power.**

Every system, mechanic, animation, sound effect, level, and boss encounter must reinforce this identity.

---

# Core Gameplay

Ash & Purr is an arcade experience where the player progresses through handcrafted levels connected by challenging boss encounters. Combat is fast, responsive, and highly readable, rewarding precision over aggression.

The player does not become significantly stronger throughout the adventure. Progression comes from learning enemy behaviors, mastering movement, making intelligent equipment choices, and improving personal skill.

Each encounter follows a rhythmic combat loop:

* Observe enemy behavior.
* Dodge incoming attacks.
* Identify openings.
* Strike with precision.
* Retreat before overcommitting.
* Repeat until victory.

Button mashing is never rewarded. Greed is consistently punished, while patience and discipline are the keys to success.

---

# Core Design Philosophy

Every gameplay decision should answer a single question:

> **Does this make the player feel like a tiny knight overcoming impossible odds through determination rather than power?**

If the answer is no, the feature should be reconsidered regardless of how technically impressive it may be.

---

# Player Experience Goals

Players should experience the following emotional progression:

### Early Game

* Curiosity
* Wonder
* Optimism
* Confidence

The world feels mysterious and beautiful, despite subtle signs that something is wrong.

### Mid Game

* Determination
* Tension
* Loneliness
* Respect for increasingly powerful opponents

The player realizes the kingdom is slowly collapsing.

### Late Game

* Desperation
* Uncertainty
* Resolve

Bosses become increasingly overwhelming while reality itself begins deteriorating.

### Final Boss

The player should not feel powerful.

The player should feel courageous.

Victory should feel earned through mastery rather than superior statistics.

---

# Combat Philosophy

Combat is inspired by Soulslike design while maintaining the immediacy and responsiveness of an arcade action game.

Key principles include:

* Precision over aggression.
* Pattern recognition over memorization.
* Skill over statistics.
* Fair difficulty.
* Complete player control.
* Instant responsiveness.

Every enemy attack must be readable.

Every player death must feel deserved.

Every victory should be memorable.

Combat should resemble a rhythmic dance where both the player and the enemy constantly exchange initiative.

---

# Player Character

**Bob** is intentionally weak compared to nearly every opponent.

He is:

* Small.
* Agile.
* Fast.
* Courageous.

His weapon is nothing more than a tree branch.

His strength never comes from equipment or overwhelming damage.

Instead, Bob succeeds because the player learns.

As the adventure progresses, Bob's abilities expand through new tools and strategic amulets, but his fundamental combat identity never changes.

He remains vulnerable from beginning to end.

---

# Boss Design Philosophy

Bosses are the centerpiece of the experience.

Every boss should immediately communicate superiority.

Their size, animations, music, attacks, arena, and introduction should establish that Bob is entering a fight against an overwhelming opponent.

Bosses should feel:

* Larger than life.
* Calm.
* Confident.
* Ancient.
* Powerful.

Each boss serves as both a gameplay challenge and an important narrative milestone.

The arena itself should reflect the boss's personality and role within the world.

---

# Difficulty Philosophy

Ash & Purr is intentionally challenging.

However, difficulty must always be fair.

Players should lose because they:

* Dodged too early.
* Attacked too greedily.
* Misread a pattern.
* Positioned poorly.
* Panicked.

Players should never lose because of:

* Unclear hitboxes.
* Hidden mechanics.
* Visual confusion.
* Unresponsive controls.
* Random behavior.

Failure is part of learning.

The game should always encourage another attempt.

---

# Progression Philosophy

The player's power progression is intentionally minimal.

Instead of constantly increasing damage numbers, progression comes from:

* Learning boss attack patterns.
* Improving execution.
* Unlocking tactical tools.
* Discovering new amulets.
* Understanding enemy behavior.

Knowledge is the primary progression system.

---

# One-Life Philosophy

The entire adventure is completed with a single life.

Every encounter carries meaningful consequences.

This rule transforms even ordinary enemies into meaningful obstacles and makes every boss encounter emotionally significant.

The player gradually develops a strong attachment to their current run, making both victory and defeat feel memorable.

---

# Amulets

Amulets provide strategic customization rather than raw power.

Every benefit introduces a meaningful drawback.

There are no universally optimal builds.

Players are encouraged to adapt their loadout based on upcoming encounters.

Preparation is considered part of mastering the game.

---

# Tools

Tools expand tactical possibilities without replacing the importance of learning enemy mechanics.

They create opportunities rather than solutions.

Proper timing is always more valuable than raw effectiveness.

---

# World Design

The kingdom is a dying world slowly consumed by corruption.

Levels become progressively more unstable as the player advances.

Environmental storytelling is prioritized over exposition.

The player should notice reality breaking long before the story explicitly explains why.

Every area should communicate history through:

* Architecture.
* Lighting.
* Enemy placement.
* Arena design.
* Music.
* Silence.

---

# Narrative Overview

Bob begins his journey with one simple objective:

Rescue his beloved cat, Almohadita.

As he defeats increasingly powerful champions, he slowly uncovers the truth:

* The kingdom is collapsing.
* Reality itself is deteriorating.
* An unknown force has manipulated the kingdom from the shadows.
* Almohadita is not an ordinary cat but the living vessel that seals a primordial force capable of reshaping existence.

Despite discovering the fate of the entire world, Bob's motivation never changes.

He is not trying to become a hero.

He is simply trying to bring his best friend home.

That unwavering simplicity is what ultimately makes him worthy of deciding the fate of reality.

---

# Artistic Direction

The visual identity is built around strong contrast.

Cute, colorful, stylized characters coexist with melancholic environments and intimidating enemies.

Visual characteristics include:

* Simple 2D shapes.
* Solid colors.
* Minimalistic facial features.
* Clean silhouettes.
* Readable animations.
* Strong contrast between the tiny protagonist and colossal bosses.

The game should feel charming at first glance while communicating danger through scale, atmosphere, and animation rather than graphic violence.

---

# Audio Direction

Sound is an essential gameplay element.

Players should learn to recognize attacks by audio alone.

Bosses have unique sound identities.

Silence is intentionally used to create tension before impactful attacks.

Music should enhance emotional pacing without reducing gameplay readability.

---

# Technical Goals

The game prioritizes responsiveness above visual complexity.

Core technical objectives include:

* Immediate player input.
* Smooth 2D movement.
* Accurate hitboxes.
* Consistent animation timing.
* Stable frame rate.
* Readable visual effects.
* Responsive camera behavior.

Every technical decision should improve gameplay clarity rather than visual spectacle.

---

# Target Player Experience

By the end of the adventure, players should not remember how much damage they dealt or what equipment they collected.

They should remember the feeling of finally defeating an enemy that once seemed impossible.

They should remember every hard-earned victory.

They should remember that courage, perseverance, and mastery—not power—allowed a tiny knight carrying nothing but a tree branch to save both his best friend and the fate of every reality.

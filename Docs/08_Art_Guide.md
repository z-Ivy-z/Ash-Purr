# Art Direction Guide

## Overview

The visual identity of **Ash & Purr** is built around one central contrast:

> **An adorable world where impossibly difficult battles take place.**

The art should immediately feel warm, charming and approachable while the gameplay communicates danger, sacrifice and determination.

Players should fall in love with the world before realizing how dangerous it truly is.

The visual style should remain consistent from beginning to end.

No realistic rendering.

No visual clutter.

Every asset must prioritize readability.

---

# Artistic Vision

The game uses a stylized 2D cartoon aesthetic inspired by modern indie games.

The objective is to make every frame readable in less than a second.

Visual simplicity is more important than visual complexity.

Everything should look handcrafted.

Every object should have a clean silhouette.

Every animation should communicate gameplay first.

---

# Core Art Pillars

## 1. Readability Above Everything

Gameplay readability always comes first.

The player should instantly recognize:

- Bob
- Bosses
- Weapons
- Hitboxes
- Interactive objects
- Projectiles

Nothing should blend into the environment.

No unnecessary visual noise.

---

## 2. Simple Shapes

Characters are built from:

- Circles
- Rounded rectangles
- Cylinders
- Soft curves

Avoid:

- Tiny decorative details
- Thin ornaments

Large readable forms are preferred.

---

## 3. Strong Silhouettes

Every important character should be recognizable using only their silhouette.

Players should identify characters even if rendered completely black.

Silhouettes must remain recognizable at small resolutions.

---

## 4. Cute, Never Childish

Characters should feel:

- Charming
- Friendly
- Warm

Never:

- Overly cartoonish
- Goofy
- Exaggerated for comedy

The world should feel sincere.

---

## 5. Contrast

The game's identity comes from contrast.

Tiny hero.

Colossal bosses.

Cute visuals.

Brutal combat.

Warm colors.

Melancholic world.

Hope surrounded by despair.

Everything should reinforce this contrast.

---

# Visual Style

The overall style should closely resemble the provided references.

Characteristics:

- Cute proportions
- Rounded armor
- Minimal facial details
- Flat colors
- Soft cel shading
- Thin clean outlines
- Highly readable silhouettes

The visual language should feel similar to a polished indie action game rather than a AAA fantasy RPG.

---

# Color Philosophy

Colors are soft and slightly desaturated.

Avoid:

- Neon colors
- Highly saturated palettes
- Heavy contrast
- Pure black shadows

The world should feel warm while still allowing darker environments later in the game.

---

# Lighting

Lighting remains extremely simple.

Use:

- Flat local colors
- One shadow color
- One highlight color

No:

- Ambient occlusion
- Global illumination
- Reflections
- Bloom-heavy effects

Lighting exists only to improve readability.

---

# Shading

Characters use:

- Flat cel shading

Each material should have:

Base Color

↓

One Shadow

↓

One Highlight

Never use gradients.

Never use soft airbrush shadows.

Every color transition should be clean.

---

# Outline Style

Outlines should be:

- Thin
- Clean
- Consistent

Avoid:

- Thick comic outlines
- Sketchy strokes
- Variable line weight

The outline exists only to separate shapes.

---

# Materials

## Metal

Armor should appear:

- Smooth
- Clean
- Rounded

Highlights are simple.

Reflections are stylized.

No scratches covering the armor.

Battle damage only appears when narratively appropriate.

---

## Cloth

Cloth has:

- Simple folds
- Large readable shapes

No realistic fabric simulation.

---

## Leather

Leather should feel:

- Soft
- Slightly worn

Avoid excessive texture.

---

## Wood

Wood (Bob's branch) should appear:

- Natural
- Warm
- Simple

The leaf should remain vibrant throughout the game.

---

# Character Design

Characters should always prioritize:

Readability

↓

Personality

↓

Animation

↓

Detail

Never the opposite.

Large shapes are preferred over tiny details.

---

# Bob

Bob represents innocence and determination.

Visual rules:

- Tiny proportions
- Large helmet
- Rounded armor
- Short cape
- Oversized gloves
- Oversized boots
- Humble branch weapon

He should always appear vulnerable.

---

# Bosses

Bosses should immediately communicate superiority.

Characteristics:

- Massive silhouettes
- Wide shoulders
- Large weapons
- Heavy armor

Bosses should occupy significantly more screen space than Bob.

Scale should communicate danger before combat begins.

---

# Bosses Design

Bob

↓

Bosses

Every enemy should have a unique silhouette.

---

# Facial Design

Bob:

No visible face.

Only darkness beneath the visor.

Bosses:

Simple expressive faces.

Large readable eyes.

Minimal facial details.

Expressions should communicate personality without becoming exaggerated.

If wearing helmets of any kind, do not remove them unless previously instructed to do so.

---

# Animation Philosophy

Animation exists to communicate gameplay.

Every movement should answer:

"What information is the player receiving?"

Beautiful animation is meaningless if gameplay becomes less readable.

---

# Animation Style

Movement should be:

- Snappy
- Responsive
- Clear

Avoid:

- Floaty movement
- Excessive anticipation
- Long recoveries

Bob's animation emphasizes agility.

---

# Effects

Visual effects should enhance impact.

Never obscure gameplay.

Use:

- Small dust clouds
- Short slash effects
- Minimal particles
- Simple impact flashes
- Small debris

Effects disappear quickly.

---

# Camera

The camera is almost invisible.

Use subtle:

- Shake
- Zoom
- Slow motion

Never excessive cinematic movement.

Gameplay always remains readable.

---

# Environment Design

Environments should feel handcrafted.

Large readable landmarks.

Simple geometry.

Minimal clutter.

Every area should support combat visibility.

---

# Environmental Progression

The world gradually deteriorates.

Early Game:

- Bright
- Peaceful
- Stable

Mid Game:

- Cracked structures
- Dead vegetation
- Darker palette

Late Game:

- Floating debris
- Reality fractures
- Distorted architecture
- Visual instability

The player should notice reality breaking before the story explains it.

---

# User Interface

The UI follows the same philosophy.

Simple.

Minimal.

Elegant.

No ornate fantasy frames.

Rounded corners.

Soft colors.

Readable typography.

Gameplay remains the focus.

---

# Resolution

Sprites should remain readable at all supported resolutions.

Small details that disappear during gameplay should be removed.

Design assets for gameplay first.

Illustrations second.

---

# Asset Quality Standard

Every asset should satisfy these questions:

- Can it be recognized instantly?
- Does it have a unique silhouette?
- Does it communicate personality?
- Does it improve gameplay readability?
- Does it match the cute fantasy aesthetic?

If the answer to any of these is **no**, the asset should be redesigned.

---

# Final Art Rule

Whenever uncertainty arises during production, every artistic decision should answer one question:

> **"Does this asset make the world feel charming while making Bob appear incredibly small against overwhelming enemies?"**

If the answer is yes, it belongs in **Ash & Purr**.

If it prioritizes realism, excessive detail or visual spectacle over readability and charm, it should be reconsidered.

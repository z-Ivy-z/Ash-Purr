# 03_Gameplay.md

# Gameplay Design Document

## Overview

**Ash & Purr** is a 2D arcade action game with combat heavily inspired by Soulslike design principles.

The game focuses on precise, deliberate combat where success comes from player mastery rather than character progression.

Players are constantly encouraged to observe, learn, adapt and persevere.

Every encounter is designed to feel fair, challenging and rewarding.

The gameplay loop revolves around:

Explore.

Discover.

Fight.

Learn.

Overcome.

Repeat.

---

# Core Gameplay Philosophy

Ash & Purr is **not** an RPG.

It is **not** a hack-and-slash.

It is **not** a button-masher.

It is a game about mastery.

The player does not become stronger.

The player becomes better.

Every mechanic reinforces the following idea:

> **Knowledge is progression.**

---

# Gameplay Pillars

Every mechanic must reinforce these pillars.

## Precision

Every attack matters.

Every dodge matters.

Every decision matters.

Button mashing should always be punished.

---

## Patience

Greed is the greatest enemy.

Players who wait for the correct opportunity are consistently rewarded.

---

## Fairness

Every enemy follows understandable rules.

Every attack is readable.

Every mistake is avoidable.

Failure always teaches something.

---

## Responsiveness

Controls should feel immediate.

Bob reacts instantly to player input.

The player should never feel delayed by the controls.

---

## Determination

Failure is expected.

Giving up is not.

The game should constantly motivate players to try again.

---

# Core Gameplay Loop

The player's experience follows a repeating cycle.

```
Reach Boss

↓

Learn Boss Patterns

↓

Defeat Boss

↓

Unlock something new

↓

Repeat
```

Each boss acts as both a narrative milestone and a mechanical exam.

---

# Combat

Combat is the foundation of the game.

It is designed around four actions.

Observe.

Dodge.

Attack.

Reposition.

Everything else supports these fundamentals.

---

# Player Movement

Bob moves quickly and precisely.

Characteristics:

- Instant acceleration.
- Immediate stopping.
- Fast direction changes.
- Excellent air control.
- High responsiveness.

Movement exists primarily for positioning during combat.

---

# Basic Attack

Bob attacks using his humble tree branch.

Characteristics:

- Fast startup.
- Short range.
- Quick recovery.
- Low damage.
- High precision.

Players should attack only after identifying safe openings.

---

# Combo System

Bob does not perform long combos.

Instead, players chain together short attacks when opportunities appear.

Typical punish windows:

- One hit.
- Two hits.
- Occasionally three hits.

Attempting additional attacks is usually unsafe.

---

# Dodge

The dodge is Bob's primary defensive mechanic.

Characteristics:

- Fast.
- Responsive.
- Contains invulnerability frames.
- Short recovery.

The dodge rewards timing rather than panic.

Rolling repeatedly should rarely be effective.

---

# Invulnerability Frames

Correctly timed dodges temporarily make Bob invulnerable.

The window should feel generous enough to reward skill while remaining strict enough to punish poor timing.

Learning enemy timing is more important than reacting quickly.

---

# Positioning

Positioning is one of the most important gameplay skills.

Players should constantly evaluate:

Distance.

Attack range.

Recovery windows.

Arena hazards.

Boss movement.

Poor positioning is often more dangerous than poor timing.

---

# Punish Windows

Every enemy has recovery periods after specific attacks.

Players are expected to recognize these openings.

Attacking outside these windows is intentionally risky.

Patience consistently leads to success.

---

# Stagger

Some attacks or tools can briefly interrupt enemies.

However:

Bosses should rarely be staggered.

When it happens, it should feel meaningful.

Stagger exists as a reward for intelligent play rather than repeated aggression.

---

# Tools

Bob can equip one combat tool before entering battle.

Tools complement combat.

They never replace it.

Proper timing determines their effectiveness.

Every tool has an independent cooldown.

---

# Amulets

Before each boss encounter, players equip one amulet.

Every amulet provides:

One advantage.

One disadvantage.

There is no universally correct choice.

Preparation is part of mastering the game.

---

# Boss Encounters

Bosses represent the highest point of gameplay.

Every boss introduces a unique mechanical challenge.

Bosses should teach players to:

Observe.

Adapt.

Remain calm under pressure.

Victory should always feel earned.

---

# Checkpoints

Checkpoints act as safe resting places.

Reaching one:

- Saves progress.
- Restores health.
- Allows equipment changes.

Enemies respawn when leaving a checkpoint.

Defeated bosses never respawn.

---

# Death

Death is an important learning mechanic.

When Bob falls:

The player immediately understands why.

The game quickly offers another attempt.

There should be almost no downtime between attempts.

Retrying should feel natural.

---

# Difficulty

Difficulty comes from:

- Timing.
- Pattern recognition.
- Positioning.
- Patience.
- Execution.
- Decision making.

Difficulty should never rely on:

Artificial health pools.

Unfair attacks.

Hidden mechanics.

Random behavior.

Poor visibility.

---

# Enemy Philosophy

Every enemy exists to teach a combat lesson.

Examples:

One enemy teaches delayed attacks.

Another teaches spacing.

Another teaches patience.

Enemies prepare players for future bosses.

---

# Boss Philosophy

Bosses combine every lesson learned previously.

Each one introduces:

New mechanics.

New timing.

New movement.

New pressure.

Players should finish every boss feeling like they improved.

---

# One-Life Mode

The adventure is designed around a single life.

Every decision carries weight.

Every boss becomes unforgettable.

Players naturally become emotionally invested in their current run.

Victory becomes significantly more meaningful.

---

# Progression

Progression is based on player mastery rather than numerical growth.

Players improve by:

Learning attack patterns.

Mastering dodge timing.

Choosing better equipment.

Improving positioning.

Understanding boss behavior.

Knowledge is the primary progression system.

---

# Replayability

Players are encouraged to replay the game.

Reasons include:

- Faster completion times.
- Better boss mastery.
- Experimenting with different amulets.
- Using different tools.

The challenge remains rewarding because success depends on skill.

---

# Accessibility

Accessibility options should improve readability without reducing challenge.

Examples:

- Adjustable UI scale.
- Camera shake intensity.
- Subtitle options.
- Key rebinding.
- Audio controls.

Difficulty should remain based on gameplay rather than hidden information.

---

# Winning the Game

The player completes the adventure by defeating the final boss and rescuing Almohadita.

The ending should feel earned because the player has mastered every mechanic introduced throughout the game.

The game should leave players with the feeling that they did not become stronger.

They became more skilled.

---

# Final Gameplay Rule

Whenever a new mechanic is introduced, ask:

> **"Does this mechanic reward player mastery rather than increasing player power?"**

If the answer is yes, it belongs in **Ash & Purr**.

If it allows players to bypass learning enemy behavior or trivializes combat, it should be redesigned.

The player's greatest upgrade should always be their own understanding of the game.

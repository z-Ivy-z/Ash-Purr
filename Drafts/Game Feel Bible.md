Core Vision

This game is not about becoming stronger.

It is about proving that courage, determination and perseverance are capable of overcoming overwhelming power.

The player never becomes the strongest warrior in the kingdom.

The player remains the smallest knight until the very end.

Every victory must feel earned.

Every boss should initially appear impossible.

The player should eventually realize that the obstacle was never statistics or equipment.

It was knowledge.

Every defeat teaches.

Every victory rewards mastery.

The game should constantly reinforce one central idea:

"A small knight carrying nothing but a tree branch can change the fate of every reality."

Pillars

Every system must reinforce these five pillars.

1. Precision

Combat rewards accuracy rather than aggression.

Every dodge matters.

Every attack matters.

Every mistake matters.

The player should never spam buttons.

Instead, every input should feel intentional.

2. Readability

Nothing should ever kill the player unfairly.

Every attack must be readable.

Every hitbox must match the animation.

Every boss should communicate exactly what it is about to do.

The player dies because of poor decisions.

Never because of confusing visuals.

3. Weight

Everything feels physical.

Every sword swing has momentum.

Every impact briefly freezes the action.

Every landing shakes the ground.

Large bosses feel enormous.

Bob feels tiny.

Nothing should feel floaty.

4. Contrast

The game's emotional identity comes from contrast.

Cute visuals.

Brutal combat.

Tiny hero.

Gigantic enemies.

Simple weapon.

Legendary opponents.

Warm memories.

Cold reality.

Hope surrounded by despair.

Everything should reinforce this duality.

5. Determination

Failure is expected.

Giving up is not.

Every mechanic should encourage trying again.

Players should always believe victory is possible.

Combat Feel

Combat is rhythmic.

Almost musical.

Players should naturally enter a flow state.

Observe.

Dodge.

Wait.

Attack.

Retreat.

Repeat.

Greed is always punished.

Patience is always rewarded.

Bob

Bob never feels powerful.

He feels capable.

His attacks are fast but light.

His movements are agile.

His size constantly reminds the player of his vulnerability.

When he defeats a giant opponent, the victory feels extraordinary because of the enormous difference in scale.

Movement

Movement should feel immediate.

Bob accelerates quickly.

Stops quickly.

Turns instantly.

Input delay should be nearly nonexistent.

Movement exists to support combat rather than exploration.

The player should always feel completely responsible for every success and every failure.

Dodge

The dodge is the player's most important defensive tool.

Its animation is fast and clean.

Invulnerability frames are generous enough to reward correct timing but short enough to punish panic rolling.

A perfectly timed dodge should feel satisfying.

The player should instinctively learn enemy timing through repetition.

Attacks

Basic attacks recover quickly.

The player should always have time to react after attacking.

Attack animations should communicate commitment without feeling sluggish.

Every hit produces:

A brief hit stop.

Small screen shake.

Metallic impact sound.

Sharp visual effect.

The player should immediately feel that contact occurred.

Bosses

Every boss is stronger than Bob.

Not only mechanically.

Visually.

Emotionally.

Narratively.

The player should feel small from the moment the boss enters the arena.

Boss introductions are slow.

Calm.

Confident.

They never rush.

Their presence alone should command respect.

Arena Feel

The arena is an extension of the boss.

Nothing feels randomly placed.

Every environment reflects its guardian.

As the game progresses, arenas slowly lose stability.

Reality itself deteriorates.

The player should subconsciously notice this before the story explains it.

Difficulty

The game is intentionally difficult.

However, difficulty is never created through unfair mechanics.

Instead it comes from:

Pattern recognition.

Positioning.

Patience.

Consistency.

Decision making.

Execution.

Every death should clearly communicate what the player could have done differently.

The player should never blame the game.

Only themselves.

The One-Life Philosophy

The player has only one life to complete the adventure.

This rule transforms every decision into a meaningful one.

Bosses become terrifying because defeat carries real consequences.

The player should gradually develop a strong emotional attachment to their current run.

Reaching a new boss should feel exciting.

Reaching a late-game boss should feel terrifying.

Victory should feel unforgettable.

Amulets

Amulets do not increase power without cost.

Every advantage introduces a weakness.

There is no objectively best choice.

Only better decisions for specific situations.

The player should think before every battle.

Preparation is part of mastery.

Tools

Tools complement combat.

They never replace it.

No tool should allow players to ignore learning boss mechanics.

Instead they create small tactical opportunities.

Correct timing is more important than raw damage.

Audio

Silence is a gameplay mechanic.

Moments before bosses attack, music should briefly step back.

Important attacks should be heard before they are seen.

Every boss has a unique sound identity.

The player should recognize attacks by sound alone.

Bob's footsteps remain quiet.

Boss footsteps shake the world.

Camera

The camera should feel invisible.

Players should never think about it.

During impactful moments:

Slight zoom.

Subtle shake.

Slow recovery.

Large attacks slightly pull the camera outward.

Critical moments briefly slow time.

Everything serves readability.

Animation

Every animation must communicate intention.

No movement exists only for visual beauty.

If a boss raises a weapon:

It is preparing an attack.

If Bob changes stance:

Recovery has finished.

Players should learn the language of animation.

Visual Effects

Effects emphasize impact.

Never obscure gameplay.

Particles disappear quickly.

Screen flashes remain subtle.

Boss attacks are always readable despite visual spectacle.

The player should never lose information because of effects.

Storytelling

The story is discovered rather than explained.

Dialogue remains minimal.

The environment tells history.

Bosses tell history.

Music tells history.

Even silence tells history.

Mystery is more powerful than exposition.

Questions should appear long before answers.

Emotional Progression

The emotional journey should evolve naturally.

Beginning

Curiosity.

Wonder.

Hope.

Middle

Determination.

Concern.

Loneliness.

Late Game

Desperation.

Uncertainty.

Acceptance.

Final Boss

Defiance.

Not because Bob believes he will win.

Because he refuses to stop trying.

Ending

Relief.

Melancholy.

Hope.

The Player's Journey

At the beginning, the player believes they are rescuing a beloved cat.

Midway through the adventure, they realize the kingdom itself is collapsing.

Near the end, they discover that Almohadita is the vessel containing a primordial force capable of reshaping existence.

Despite this revelation, Bob's motivation never changes.

He is not fighting to save the world.

He is fighting to bring his best friend home.

That unwavering simplicity is what makes him worthy of carrying the fate of every reality.

Final Design Rule

Whenever uncertainty arises during development, every decision should answer one question:

"Does this make the player feel like a tiny knight overcoming impossible odds through determination rather than power?"

If the answer is yes, the decision aligns with the identity of the game.

If the answer is no, it should be reconsidered, no matter how impressive or technically advanced the feature may seem.
